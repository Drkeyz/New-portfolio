<Section n="01" title="Context" headline="Why use five apps *when one will do?*">

<Lead>Cargolandfood entered a market already crowded with food delivery: Jumia Food, Chowdeck, Glovo, UberEats, Bolt Food.</Lead>

Most of them ported global playbooks into Nigeria with little local adaptation, and none solved for the daily reality that **food, groceries, pharmacy runs and drinks all happen in the same week**.

So the brief wasn't to design another food app. It was a **complete neighbourhood marketplace**: food-first, but reaching into everything Nigerians actually order each week.

<Statement text="Built for foodies who need *everything,* not shoppers who want food." cite="Cargolandfood's positioning" />

</Section>

<Section n="02" title="Problem" headline="One marketplace, *three* broken experiences.">

<Lead>The market was fragmented, unreliable and inefficient for every side of it at once.</Lead>

Designing for one side meant designing for all three in parallel. Customers want speed and reliability, restaurant partners need order management and growth, and riders need transparent earnings and efficient routes. Each group has its own pain points, mental model and definition of success.

  items={[

<Lead>The answer, side by side: transparent pricing, vendor quality signals, ETAs that update mid-trip, single-flow restaurant onboarding, and a per-trip earnings ledger for riders.</Lead>

</Section>

<Section n="05" title="Design" headline="Fast enough to keep *hungry* users.">

<Sub kicker="Onboarding">The bare minimum, and nothing more.</Sub>

Onboarding is the first contact with the product, so it carries the value proposition and gets out of the way. Three illustrated screens set expectations, then sign-up asks only for what a first order needs, with the address chosen in one tap from your current location. Guests can skip straight in.

<Sub kicker="The system">100+ screens. Two themes. One system.</Sub>

Cart, favourites, coupons, biometric sign-in, empty states, restaurant pages, the drinks and market verticals: each ships in light and dark from the same component library. Dark mode isn't an inversion. It was tuned for low light, with deeper contrast on actions and softer surfaces everywhere else.
