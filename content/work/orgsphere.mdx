<Section n="01" title="Context" headline="From paper-heavy HR *to a single app.*">

<Lead>The brief: replace fragmented, paper-heavy HR processes with a modern workspace that puts employees in control of their working lives.</Lead>

Leave requests lived in emails. Expense claims travelled on paper. Performance reviews surfaced once a year, opaque and disconnected from day-to-day work. Company events had no central home, and turnout suffered for it.

The opportunity was a **single mobile app** for all of it. Not enterprise software wearing a friendly face, but a workspace built around the employee first, with managers and HR served by the same shared logic.

</Section>

<Section n="02" title="Problem" headline="Four broken patterns. *Four direct fixes.*">

<Section n="03" title="Research" headline="One app, *three* distinct lenses.">

Stakeholder interviews with HR leads at Chams Access, employee surveys, and an audit of HR apps such as BambooHR and Keka. Three groups use the app, each with different goals, so the design shares one set of patterns but tunes density, primary actions and approval flows per role.

<Section n="04" title="Thinking" headline="Learn one module, *know them all.*">

Five stages, each validating the last before the next began: research, information architecture for the leave, expense, KPI and activity flows, low-fidelity wireframes tested internally, high-fidelity design, then interactive prototypes and an annotated developer handoff.

The principle that shaped every screen came out of the architecture stage.

<Statement
  text="Once you learn how leave works, you *already know* how expenses work. The same pattern carries you through every module."
  cite="Core design principle · consistency first"
/>

</Section>

<Sub kicker="Performance">Quarterly KPIs, made transparent.</Sub>

Employees self-assess, see their manager's scores and track progress across the year. The flow moves through four states: every quarter at a glance, the review split into objectives and skills (weighted 80/20), a one-to-five rating, and the completed review with comments from both sides.
